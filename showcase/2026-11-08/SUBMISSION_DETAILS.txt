Project title: The Tests Passed. The Grader Said No.
Subtitle: Checking the checker in AI security repair
Team members: Imran Zakirov; Iosifova Nuriya; Kramich Anna
Mentor (also active team member): Kramich Anna

Abstract (140 words):
Can we judge when an AI should stop repairing code if its evaluator is unreliable? We audited 40 saved sessions from one local language model across five small Python security task types. In 20 sessions, our benchmark applied a hidden rule that rejected every candidate. Nine final candidates passed the implemented tests but still received FAIL. Separately, 14 sessions requested termination after a public PASS. Thirteen later ended through the runner's self-termination path, with a median 128-second delay after acknowledgement; one timed out. No further repair attempts were logged. A scripted control confirmed that the old runner required a separate final stop signal. These findings expose limits in our evaluation system, not proof of insecure code or AI stubbornness. We improved logging and tested immediate stopping in the newer collector. Broader behavioral claims need independently reviewed tasks and further experiments.
