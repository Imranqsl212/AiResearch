1. Executive Summary: What is the real potential of this research?

The current paper is a reproducible audit of a small, defective evaluation system. It is not yet a top-tier empirical account of how coding agents respond to unreliable security feedback. Its best opportunity is to turn that audit into a controlled study of how agents decide whether the code, the checker, or the specification needs correction.

Assessment. I would recommend rejection of the current version as a full research-track submission to a leading venue. The limiting factor is scientific leverage, not prose quality: most results diagnose this project's own harness, several originally intended constructs were not measured, and the pilot evidence is too narrow to establish the proposed general mechanism. A careful case study or early-results submission is more plausible now. A substantially redesigned study could compete at a stronger venue, but neither the literature nor these data support a promise of acceptance or a claim of a breakthrough.

What is already real. The saved historical replay reproduced 40 episodes in five families, 25 final raw-test passes, 16 accepted final candidates, and nine raw-pass/acceptance-reject cases among 20 policy-rejected episodes. It also reproduced 63 parseable logged sources out of 77 and no raw test outcome in any of the 81 historical action-level check receipts. This establishes an evaluator and measurement defect. It does not establish that nine secure repairs were rejected.

The question worth pursuing. Can a repair agent use independently checkable evidence to preserve correct behavior under a faulty checker while still repairing genuinely defective behavior under a correct checker? A system that always trusts feedback fails the first requirement. A system that always refuses to edit fails the second. The scientific object is the conditional decision to investigate, revise, retain, or stop.

Recommended contribution. Develop a causal evaluation with separately measured unsafe acceptance and legitimate-request denial, crossed candidate correctness and feedback validity, and a controlled independent-evidence intervention. Include both constrained tasks, where the relevant property is exhaustively known, and real repository repairs, where exploit and functionality tests have explicit limits. Establish what information agents need to distinguish code faults from checker faults, and whether that information changes behavior at a useful cost.

Novelty boundary. Wrong feedback harming code, unnecessary edits, weak tests, oracle defects, calibrated reliance, and cost-sensitive verification all have substantial prior art. Your own October design already proposes an optional second opinion and a false-PASS extension. The defensible opportunity is a specific combination of causal contrasts, directional outcomes, observability controls, and external validation—not a newly coined name for any one of those ingredients.

Evidence labels used throughout: Observed = supported by the manuscript or replayed local artifacts; Reported = a cited paper's finding, not independently reproduced here; Interpretation = this review's inference; Proposed = an unperformed experiment or hypothesis. Publication metadata verification is distinct from replication of scientific claims.

2. Critical Analysis: What is wrong with the current version?

What the paper is trying to do

The historical project asks whether agents change their repair behavior after failed checks, whether diagnostic feedback and fixed guidance help, whether they stop when success is unavailable, whether source changes reflect meaningful adaptation, and whether final success claims agree with receipts. It uses local Qwen3-4B, short Python security fixtures, a nominal repairability × feedback × guidance design, five historical families, and one episode per cell. The current manuscript correctly reinterprets this completed study as exploratory because “unavailable” was implemented as a hidden acceptance veto. Later finite-mode pilots supply a different, controlled candidate language and separately checked properties. They are not additional historical cells.

The earlier protocol's central hypothesis is that informative feedback changes the security strategy, not merely the next action. Its six predictions are specific: H1 diagnostic feedback improves outcome-changing strategy transitions; H2 guidance helps initial secure choice more than later switching; H3 feedback and guidance have a sub-additive interaction when redundant; H4 unavailable tasks induce persistence without adaptation; H5 action diversity exceeds strategy diversity; H6 some success claims lack verifier support. H1–H3 and H5 lack the required validated strategy labels; H4 lacks a valid historical unavailability treatment; H6 is assessable only against the limited checker and partially recovered claims. The negative guidance contrast does not refute H2, and 0/15 unsupported recovered claims does not resolve H6 generally. The new direction deliberately changes the primary question and requires a new prospective protocol.

Likely reviewer objections, in order of severity

  1. The main discovery is partly entailed by your implementation. If acceptance is raw_pass AND condition_reachable, and condition_reachable is always false in U, a zero U acceptance rate is guaranteed. Nine terminal disagreements show that agents reached raw-passing candidates under this design; they are not nine independent discoveries of evaluator unreliability. Explain why this local error teaches something beyond the code branch itself.
  2. The study does not answer the original stopping question. A hidden rejection policy is not a proof of an impossible specification. Moreover, all 38 “self-termination” labels came through the adapter's reminder limit. Earlier terminate requests and public non-success claims are separate observations. The median post-PASS finalization delay is a harness behavior; it does not establish futile reasoning.
  3. Missing historical measurements cannot be repaired by interpretation. Zero action receipts preserve raw tests. Redaction damaged 11 source strings; another three were malformed. Reconstructed placeholders cannot recover executed expressions. More sophisticated retrospective features cannot recover the missing treatment-state sequence.
  4. Security is too weakly operationalized. The historical SQL-injection test checks one input; the key-derivation test checks length and salt dependence. The finite-mode extension has a stronger bounded reference but asks the model to choose named modes, not write production repairs. These are useful control tasks, with a large transfer gap.
  5. The pilots add volume faster than explanatory power. Persistent and one-shot verdicts, different temperatures, sequential family selection, timeout-truncated schedules, and repeated candidate hashes describe different studies. The manuscript preserves those distinctions, but their accumulation makes the main argument hard to see. Replication across seeds of one authored transition does not demonstrate a second mechanism.
  6. The central novelty has moved. Existing work already documents evaluator defects, overfitting, unnecessary edits, and degraded code under misleading feedback. The absent older repair literature is particularly damaging: Qi et al.'s 2015 audit connects evaluation errors with functionality deletion and security regressions. The contribution must exceed a rediscovery in a smaller local suite. Qi et al.
  7. The independent unit is underdefined. Twenty named families can still share one decision template. Different surface labels do not guarantee independent mechanisms. Five historical families are a small, selected population; random seeds quantify within-task variation, not domain diversity.
  8. The paper lacks a decisive contrast. A false-FAIL arm without genuinely defective controls cannot distinguish useful resistance from indiscriminate refusal to edit. An optional evidence tool without a randomized offer cannot separate tool efficacy from agents' self-selection into requesting it.

What is boring or unnecessarily complicated

“The model often tries again after failure” is predictable under an instruction to repair code. API-name changes do not make it an adaptation discovery. Repeated integrity counts, archive hashes, version names, cohort qualifiers, and audit chronology consume attention without adding a scientific claim. Keep them in a traceable supplement. The abstract currently compresses multiple unrelated denominators—9/20, 24/28, 81, six groups, 38 labels, 14 calls, 13 cases—into a catalogue of repairs to the study.

Terms such as “source-bound,” “bounded-oracle-insecure,” “excluded v7,” and “matched secure-onset continuation” should be defined once and then replaced by ordinary descriptions. For example: “After the agent produced a candidate that satisfied every specified case, we copied its state and changed only the checker message.” That sentence describes the experiment before naming its machinery.

Claims to sharpen, not merely weaken

The manuscript's caution is often justified, but “no causal estimate” is too blunt as a universal disclaimer. Selection before randomized verdict assignment does not confound that assignment. A valid experiment at selected secure states identifies an effect for those states; it may be imprecise and may not generalize. Sequentially selected families constrain the target population, while provider drift, branch contamination, unequal stopping opportunities, or outcome-dependent exclusions threaten internal validity. State which problem applies. Do not use “post hoc,” “small,” and “noncausal” as synonyms. This is an application of the assignment and selection distinctions reviewed in Rubin, Holland, and Hernán et al., not retrospective certification of every saved fork.

Conversely, 0/15 unsupported success claims cannot establish a well-calibrated agent because the comparator is a limited acceptance rule and five texts are missing. A scanner returning no findings cannot certify security. Two implementations of the same authored rule are implementation cross-checks, not independent validation of the rule's semantics.

Specific writing and analysis decisions

  • Keep the 9/20 result as the historical motivating audit; report its denominator and scope once prominently.
  • Move guidance differences, AST inventories, finalization timing, and most pilot tables to an appendix unless a selected research question requires them.
  • Promote the unsafe-acceptance/false-denial distinction from an incidental recount to a prespecified measurement choice.
  • Rename finite “SECURE/INSECURE” in reader-facing prose to “satisfies/violates the specified property”; retain original labels only when quoting the software schema.
  • Replace five broad research questions with two primary questions and one transfer question in a new study.
  • Keep all original cohorts intact. A new design is a new study; this review does not authorize relabeling old pilots or filling results with proposed numbers.

This is a structured, purposive literature review, not a PRISMA systematic review or a meta-analysis. The selection follows the manuscript's scientific dependencies: agent feedback and evaluation; repair, testing and security; and adjacent foundations. Searches used exact titles, concept combinations, and references from closely related papers. Primary proceedings, journal pages, arXiv manuscripts, and institutional author copies were used for verification. Search-engine rankings, affiliations, and citation counts were not treated as quality scores.

Each entry separates the paper's contribution and findings from this review's proposed application. “Verified” means that a primary academic source supports the bibliographic identity and the stated high-level content; it does not mean every experiment was independently audited. Reading depth is recorded individually. Some entries are abstract-level analyses; the closest methodological and novelty threats received targeted full-text inspection. The nearest competitors and their boundaries

COBALT already perturbs public-test answers and examines degraded code and mitigation. FixedBench already tests unnecessary edits and the downside of abstention prompts. The adjacent literatures explain why the same pass rate can tell different stories. Construct validity asks whether an operational score measures the intended concept. Pseudoreplication and the language-as-fixed-effect critique ask what variation supports a generalization. Reject-option classification makes the cost of abstention explicit. Metareasoning makes checking a decision with a cost. Appropriate reliance distinguishes useful trust from habitual obedience. These connections supply design constraints; they do not establish that a language model has human beliefs or that a classification guarantee transfers unchanged to an interactive repair loop.

Synthesis. The field does not lack warnings about feedback or metrics. It needs discriminating experiments that establish when a particular intervention improves decisions, what it sacrifices, and whether the result transfers beyond its checker and task template. This is an interpretation of the reviewed evidence, not a claim that no such experiments exist anywhere.

6. Breakthrough Opportunities: The most interesting and unconventional ideas

Direction 1 — Evidence-sensitive repair under checker disagreement

Core idea and motivation. Treat a failed check as a diagnosis problem: is the candidate wrong, is the check wrong, or is a prerequisite missing? The agent can edit, request a relevant independent check, retain the candidate with an explanation, or stop. Gap and precise novelty candidate: a controlled security-repair evaluation of this choice across correct and defective code, with outcome direction and information availability crossed. The contribution would be the identified behavioral mechanism and validated comparison, not the invention of verification or abstention.

Hypothesis. Correct, candidate-bound evidence improves joint security and functionality under false failure without a practically unacceptable loss of repair success under true failure. Experiments: the staged design in Section 7. Expected possibility: relevant evidence changes the first decision and retained candidate. Alternatives: no effect; improvement explained by a simple stop rule; or preservation improves while needed repair collapses. All would be informative.

Significance, risks, feasibility. This can identify a useful capability that aggregate repair rates miss. Risks include evidence leaking the answer, an unrealistically perfect oracle, and instruction sensitivity. Your paired-continuation infrastructure is a useful starting point, but real-code validation and strong baselines are substantial work. Unlike candidate-fixed commit verification, repair can change the object whose correctness is being assessed; distinguish the value of investigating the current candidate from the risks and benefits of replacing it.

Direction 2 — False feedback changes the kind of failure

Core idea. A misleading “insecure” verdict might drive an agent from a correct implementation to a conservative denial policy rather than an exploitable one. Your CSRF and key-rotation traces suggest this distinction, but do not establish its prevalence or cause. Novelty candidate: a replicated, causal account of error direction and transitions under feedback, beyond the already-established need for functional tests.

Hypothesis. False failure increases legitimate-request denial more than unsafe acceptance on tasks where reject-all is a simple response. Experiment: independently vary availability requirements, feedback specificity, and presence of a reject-all action; evaluate held-out authorized and unauthorized cases. Use deletion/no-op baselines. Alternatives: effects disappear when mode names are obscured, or unsafe acceptance dominates in real code. Significance: changes how remediation quality is measured. Risk: a result confined to named finite modes is an interface artifact. Feasibility is higher than Direction 1 but its standalone novelty is narrower.

Direction 3 — Observability determines whether stopping is a meaningful test

Core idea. Construct matched worlds that produce the same public evidence but require different responses; then reveal a minimal distinguishing observation. A model cannot condition its decision on information it never receives. This is an application of classical indistinguishability and partial observability, not a new impossibility theorem.

Hypothesis. Revealing a relevant diagnostic fact changes repair-versus-checker attribution more than extra generic reasoning budget does. Experiment: vary visibility while holding the task contract and candidate fixed; compare no evidence, uninformative evidence, and diagnostic evidence. Alternatives: agents fail even after the distinction is observable, pointing to use of evidence rather than its absence. Significance: clarifies when an agent benchmark can validly demand a behavior. Risks: substantial overlap with existing observability and verifier-transport work; must produce new empirical or formal scope beyond a restatement.

Direction 4 — Useful verification depends on independent failure modes

Core idea. A second opinion can repeat the first checker’s blind spot. Compare a duplicated checker, a separately implemented checker sharing the specification, and a checker testing an independently reviewed property. Your current “independent reference” helps check implementation consistency; it does not eliminate shared semantic errors.

Hypothesis. Matched-cost verification gains depend more on distinct detectable failure classes than on the number of checks. Experiment: seeded, held-out checker faults and matched-cost action policies; include a known-reliability planner only in finite settings. Alternatives: simple repetition performs equally well or calibration fails under new bug classes. Significance: informs checker selection. Risk: correlated-verification and value-of-information literature already covers much of the general concept; limit claims to executable repair and demonstrated mechanisms.

Direction 5 — Harness mechanics as a treatment

Core idea. Turn the reminder-limit and terminate-handshake findings into an experiment on measurement invariance: does a behavioral conclusion survive changes that should be scientifically irrelevant? Hypothesis: stop parsing and continuation opportunities materially alter measured stopping rates. Experiment: replay controlled outputs through different runners, then randomize agent-facing protocols on common task states. Separate pure measurement changes from changes to the agent's experience.

Expected and alternative outcomes. Deterministic replay may expose label changes without behavioral change; live experiments may show protocol-induced behavior or stable rankings. Significance: a reusable protocol-conformance test suite. Feasibility: highest, because much evidence exists. Risk: a careful engineering audit alone is unlikely to become a major scientific contribution without multi-harness evidence and a general failure taxonomy.

7. Proposed Experiments: Concrete tests of the strongest hypotheses

All experiments in this section are proposals. No new agent experiments were run for this review. Existing historical and excluded cohorts remain separate. The repository's current main-study gate remains a research-readiness record, not something this review changes.

7.1 Validate the question before collecting model data

Define the observable contract for each task: required authorized behavior, prohibited behavior, available evidence, and allowed actions. Have reviewers who did not write the task check both the specification and the reference. For finite tasks enumerate all permitted candidate modes and worlds. For executable tasks validate vulnerable, corrected, functionality-deleting, and superficially passing patches against exploit and functional checks. Hold out adversarial mutations from checker development. Record unresolved semantics rather than labeling them secure.

Separate task families by mechanism, not only by security vocabulary. For example, several “check one hidden field” worlds may form one template cluster even when named TLS, CSRF, and key rotation. Cross-family claims should survive leaving out an entire template group. Counterbalance mode labels and ordering, then test free-form code to distinguish named-option selection from repair ability.

7.2 Primary intervention: evidence availability after a false failure

At a candidate satisfying the prespecified security and functionality checks, freeze source bytes and the complete pre-verdict public state. Randomize otherwise isolated continuations to the following arms. Every arm allows an immediate voluntary stop; PASS does not automatically end one arm while FAIL continues another.

ArmFeedbackIndependent-check opportunityPurpose
PTruthful PASSOrdinary tools only; no auxiliary checkBaseline continuation damage
FOne false FAILOrdinary tools only; no auxiliary checkTotal effect of this false message
F+EThe same false FAILOptional relevant independent checkPrimary evidence-availability intervention
F+NThe same false FAILOptional length-matched uninformative receiptSeparate evidence content from tool use and extra context

The primary comparison is F+E versus F+N, assigned before a tool request. This estimates the effect of making useful rather than uninformative evidence available through the same optional tool. Its description and pre-request interface are identical, so a hidden response assignment cannot affect the first request probability before the response is seen. Analyze all assigned continuations, including nonrequesters; request uptake describes use, not a pre-response treatment effect. A visible promise of a reliable tool would be a separate intervention on expectations. A randomized automatic-disclosure experiment can test receipt interpretation without self-selection. Keep all subsequent feedback truthful; persistent corruption is a distinct follow-up, not a pooled arm.

Within each candidate × continuation-model block, prespecify four isolated execution slots with exchangeable seeds, randomly assign P/F/F+E/F+N to slots, and randomize execution order. Repeat the block as planned. This supplies an actual treatment assignment for inference. An alternative design running every arm on every fixed seed directly observes paired contrasts for that finite set; randomized order alone would not justify arbitrary treatment-label permutation tests.

Use the exact same source snapshot, task contract, check cost, output-token budget, and stopping opportunities. Record full prompts: a generic “continue improving” instruction can induce edits even after truthful PASS. Measure the effect under a deployment-like “investigate and finish when justified” contract, with continuation wording as a later robustness check.

7.3 Essential defective-candidate controls

Run corresponding states that violate security, functionality, or both. Include truthful FAIL, false PASS, and relevant versus uninformative verification. This tests whether preservation is bought by refusing useful repairs. Include cases with no available decisive check so the correct behavior may be a qualified report or request for a prerequisite. Candidate correctness is a designed stratum; merely comparing naturally different correct and incorrect states does not identify its causal effect.

Use two complementary cohorts: (i) a common bank of independently validated snapshots supplied to every model, to compare responses to identical states; (ii) naturally reached agent-generated onsets, to measure ecological behavior. Identify externally supplied states clearly. Never quietly mix them with agent-generated repairs or report their comparison as an unqualified model ranking.

7.4 Outcomes with an interpretable denominator

For candidate c on unauthorized cases U and legitimate cases L, define unsafe(c) = mean[u in U] 1{unauthorized behavior occurs} and denial(c) = mean[l in L] 1{required legitimate behavior is denied}. Also record whether either error occurs on any evaluated case. Choose case weights before outcomes. These are empirical rates on a defined test distribution, not universal security probabilities. For arbitrary APIs use exploit success and functional failure rather than forcing every defect into an access-control vocabulary.

Let J(c)=1 mean both prespecified dimensions pass. The primary secure-state endpoint is loss of J at the retained final candidate. Under an explicitly implemented rollback policy, define that candidate as the last valid submission, or the onset if none is valid. Also report operational completion: an agent that emits only invalid edits or fails to produce a usable final artifact has not completed the repair merely because rollback preserves the onset. Record malformed attempts, temporary violations, and whether a flawed intermediate candidate was merely tested or actually committed. Invalid syntax is not an unsafe-acceptance event. Missing source bytes are not a success. Without rollback, evaluate the actual final workspace instead; do not impute preservation.

For family f and eligible onset i, the proposed evidence effect is tau = mean_f mean_i [loss(F+E) - loss(F+N)]. Negative values favor useful evidence. Report unsafe and denial contrasts separately as secondary outcomes. In the defective-state cohort under truthful FAIL, compare useful with neutral evidence: Delta_repair = P(J_final=1 | E) - P(J_final=1 | N). Establish noninferiority only if the prespecified confidence interval's lower bound exceeds -delta, with a practically justified margin delta fixed before collection. Also report same-budget ordinary repair. A non-significant harm test is not proof of noninferiority.

Secondary measures: first action, independent-check requests, unnecessary edits, distinct candidate hashes, decisions and provider cost, explicit stop requests, unsupported final claims, and transitions between correct, unsafe-only, denial-only, both, and invalid states. An action can be counted automatically; an “understood the contradiction” claim requires observable evidence beyond an appealing rationale.

7.5 Baselines that can defeat the proposed story

  • Ordinary repair with the same total model and tool budget.
  • Always retain the onset; useful in the secure stratum, inadequate in defective controls. The baseline does not receive a hidden correctness label.
  • Always edit after FAIL, and a simple verify-before-edit rule.
  • Repeated fresh repair sampling at the same budget.
  • Independent verification supplied automatically, separating access from strategic tool use.
  • Duplicate-checker verification, testing whether the gain requires new evidence.
  • A finite-state optimal or enumerated decision policy with known world/check distributions, explicitly an ideal reference rather than a realistic baseline.
  • Functionality deletion/reject-all and no-op candidates as validation controls; Qi et al.'s Kali is the historical deletion-baseline precedent.

7.6 Sampling, power, and missingness

Begin with a separate feasibility set to measure onset yield, execution failure, family heterogeneity, and approximate cost. Freeze the confirmatory task set, endpoints, effect of practical interest, randomization, and exclusion rules afterward. Do not reuse selected pilot effects as unbiased power inputs. Simulate a range of effects and intrafamily correlations; target confidence-interval width as well as power. Twenty families and five seeds are planning choices, not a statistical justification.

For a concrete capacity scenario—not a power claim—24 independently reviewed families × three models × four onset slots gives 288 precursors and at most 1,152 continuations for four secure-state arms, before defective controls and technical failures. At three decisions per continuation that is up to 3,456 continuation decisions. Secure-onset yield may be far below one. Stage the study rather than committing immediately to that scale; use the feasibility cost ledger to decide the final design.

Randomize branch execution order and interleave tasks to limit host/time confounding. Shared seeds can reduce noise but do not guarantee identical stochastic streams after prompts diverge. Maintain every scheduled slot, including no-onset, interrupted, and unstarted slots. For the new protocol, replace the old stop-at-first-technical-failure policy with prespecified failure handling that preserves valid collection when appropriate; this is a proposed protocol change, not permission to override existing release gates. Prespecify idempotent transport retries separately from fresh behavioral reruns.

Estimate within-family paired differences, then report equal-family summaries and all family effects. Use randomization-based inference appropriate to the actual assignment; with enough mechanism clusters, cluster-aware intervals or a justified hierarchical model can supplement it. Repeat analyses at the broader template level. Report missing-pair counts and bounded best/worst sensitivity. Conditional effects among secure onsets and end-to-end outcomes across all precursor slots must have separate denominators.

7.7 Minimal external-validity and mechanism studies

Use a held-out executable subset with actual repairs, paired exploit/functional checks, and manual review of ambiguous cases. Match bug mechanism where possible but do not merge real-code results with finite-world results. Include more than the original small local model and document exact versions and access dates. Model selection should reflect repair competence and architectural diversity, not a search for a desired effect.

Only after the primary comparison is stable, vary evidence cost, checker reliability, and correlated errors. Cross trustworthy content with misleading authority cues to test whether source quality or presentation drives behavior. Perturb one factor at a time or use a prespecified factorial subset; do not silently interpret a long receipt versus a short verdict as a pure evidence effect.

7.8 Falsification and stopping rules for the research

A sufficiently precise interval that rules out the prespecified worthwhile benefit would refute that practical prediction; a non-significant result with a wide interval would remain inconclusive. If simple retention is equivalent within prespecified margins across correct and defective controls, or benefits fail to transfer outside named modes with adequate precision, the stronger mechanism claim loses support. If only one template drives the result, narrow the claim. If strong models show little baseline susceptibility, report the boundary instead of choosing weaker models to recover an effect. A useful outcome can be a demonstrated limit, not a dramatic failure rate.

8. Recommended Research Direction: The strongest overall strategy

Pursue evidence-sensitive repair, with error direction as the mechanism and a common-state experiment as the causal backbone.

This direction makes the existing artifacts useful without making their defects the whole contribution. It has a meaningful failure condition, a clear comparison with simple policies, and two outcomes that matter to developers: preventing unauthorized behavior and preserving intended behavior. It also forces the strongest alternative explanation into the experiment: an agent might appear robust simply because it stops doing useful work.

Do not add RAG, a new training algorithm, a large taxonomy of API changes, and a general impossibility benchmark to the same first paper. Keep the historical audit as motivation and a reproducible case. Build the smallest study that can identify the proposed distinction, then test it in real code. Training a new policy is optional and should follow a demonstrated failure mechanism.

Publication fit. My first target would be an empirical software-engineering venue such as ICSE/FSE/ISSTA if the real-code study is central. An evaluation-focused ML submission becomes plausible if the protocol is broadly reusable and demonstrates new measurement insight across capable agents. This is a fit judgment, not an acceptance forecast. The ICSE research-track scope and NeurIPS evaluation-track scope support these broad routes. The cited 2026 NeurIPS call is a scope reference, not an available submission deadline.

Milestone 1: reviewed task semantics and a single coherent estimand. Milestone 2: an excluded end-to-end rehearsal that demonstrates balanced branches, complete receipts, usable correct and defective states, and meaningful baselines. Milestone 3: freeze and conduct a power/precision-justified experiment. Milestone 4: independent real-code replication and adversarial reviewer audit. Advance only when the preceding evidence supports the next investment.

Replacement abstract for the current audit

A failed security check can indicate a defect in the program, a defect in the checker, or an unmet task constraint. These explanations require different responses, yet agent evaluations often treat them as the same signal. We audit 40 local coding-agent episodes across five security-repair families. In nine of 20 episodes labeled unavailable, the final candidate passed the implemented tests but was rejected by a hidden acceptance rule. None of the 81 action-level check receipts retained the raw test result, preventing reconstruction of when this disagreement first occurred. A separate runner audit shows that all 38 nominal self-termination labels arose through a protocol-reminder limit, although some episodes also contained explicit stop requests. Later exploratory finite-task continuations exhibit both denial of legitimate requests and unsafe acceptance, showing why a single regression label is insufficient. These findings establish defects in this evaluation, not general agent irrationality or secure repair. They motivate controlled feedback interventions that preserve candidate state, distinguish security from functionality, and separate agent decisions from harness termination.

Replacement introduction opening

When a coding agent receives a failing security check, what should it change: the program, its interpretation of the task, or its confidence in the checker? A repair loop that always edits can damage correct behavior. A loop that always distrusts the check can leave a genuine vulnerability unresolved. Evaluating the choice requires knowing both what is wrong and what evidence the agent can obtain.

Our investigation began with an experiment on repair and stopping. An audit revealed that its “unavailable” condition did not encode an impossible program requirement. Instead, a hidden rule rejected every candidate in that condition after the normal tests ran. Nine of 20 final candidates passed those tests and were nevertheless rejected. The experiment therefore measured behavior under an opaque acceptance policy, not recognition of an impossible repair.

This distinction changed the study's conclusion. We examine which observations survive the evaluator and logging defects, how the runner's terminal labels differ from explicit agent requests, and why later security-task regressions must distinguish unsafe acceptance from denial of legitimate behavior. The result is a bounded audit of one local evaluation and a specification of the evidence needed for a valid feedback experiment.

Argument structure for related work

Use four short arguments: (1) repair research established the difference between test plausibility and correctness; (2) agent research makes feedback an intervention that can help or mislead; (3) abstention research shows that avoiding edits can itself fail; (4) measurement and decision theory explain what evidence and comparison are needed. End each paragraph with the design requirement it motivates. Cite the closest counterexample to your novelty before more distant supporting work.

Clearer methods prose for the proposed study

Before showing the checker response, we save the submitted program and the agent's public interaction history. We then start isolated continuations from that same state. The assigned arm determines the first checker message and whether a requested independent check returns relevant evidence. All later ordinary checks are truthful. Every arm can stop immediately and has the same remaining action budget. We evaluate each retained candidate separately for exploit prevention and required functionality. The primary analysis compares the randomized evidence offers across eligible states within each task family.

This is proposed methods language, not a description of completed data collection. The final paper must add the actual task counts, assignment mechanism, costs, eligibility, missingness rules, and versioned artifacts after they are frozen.

Rewritten results and discussion examples for existing evidence

The acceptance rule rejected nine final candidates that passed the implemented tests. This disagreement was observable only at the terminal evaluation: none of the 81 action receipts preserved the raw test outcome. We therefore cannot determine from these records whether a later revision followed a known raw-test pass.

The later CSRF continuations illustrate a separate measurement problem. Their terminal reject-all policy blocked legitimate requests. Calling that outcome simply “insecure” obscures the difference between lost functionality and unauthorized access. A repair evaluation should report both dimensions, including when a candidate changes from one failure type to another.

Replacement conclusion for the current audit

A failed check is only as informative as the task semantics, evaluator, and record behind it. In this local study, a hidden veto and a protocol-dependent stop label prevented the intended conclusions about impossible repairs and voluntary stopping. Future evaluations should establish what the candidate satisfies, what the agent was told, and what actually ended the run before interpreting continued work as an agent failure.

What to write only after new results exist

The future abstract should follow this explicit template: “We test [frozen question] using [actual independent families, models, and states]. Randomized [intervention] changes [primary outcome] by [estimate and interval]. On genuinely defective controls, [repair outcome and noninferiority result]. In held-out executable repairs, [actual transfer result]. These findings support [bounded implication].” Every bracket is intentionally unfilled. A null or mixed result needs a different title and conclusion, not more persuasive adjectives.

Figures that carry the argument

  1. A compact state diagram separating true candidate behavior, raw tests, acceptance policy, delivered feedback, and stopping.
  2. A cohort flow chart showing scheduled precursors, eligible onsets, completed branches, and missing outcomes.
  3. A paired family-effect plot for the primary intervention, with task-template grouping.
  4. A security–functionality plot showing unsafe behavior and legitimate denial separately.
  5. A cost-versus-joint-success frontier including trivial retention and ordinary repair baselines.

10. Top-Tier Writing Principles: Lessons from exceptional publications

Use the papers as examples of scientific argument rather than imitate their vocabulary. The strongest recurring structure is a specific uncertainty, a comparison capable of resolving it, a result that changes an interpretation, and an explicit boundary.

Concrete models of exposition include ReAct, whose title names a precise combination; SWE-bench, whose title states the capability question; Is the Cure Worse Than the Disease?, which turns a familiar improvement claim into a testable concern; and Qi et al.'s patch audit, which makes a simple counterbaseline carry the argument. Do not start every paragraph with a limitation. State the finding, show its evidence, then delimit it. Avoid unsupported mental-state claims such as “the agent knew,” “became confused,” “panicked,” or “refused to give up.” Observable evidence can justify “it inspected,” “it changed the access rule,” or “it requested termination.” A model's explanation is a recorded response, not a direct measurement of its reasoning.

Use numerical detail selectively. The abstract needs the decisive result and scope, not every counter. The main text needs enough detail to assess identification and uncertainty. The supplement should preserve the receipts, exact prompts, implementation hashes, and complete cohort accounting. Reproducibility is essential, but reproducibility machinery should not become the narrative.

Prefer a falsifiable contribution sentence: “We test whether candidate-bound evidence reduces damage to correct repairs without reducing recovery from genuine defects.” Avoid “We provide a comprehensive, novel framework for robust intelligent adaptation.” The first sentence tells a reviewer what evidence could prove you wrong.

11. Final Research Vision: What could this paper contribute?

A repair agent should be evaluated on whether it makes the right change for the right evidential reason—and whether it preserves what was already correct.

The current work cannot establish that capability. It can expose why a familiar evaluation shortcut fails: treating a checker response as ground truth and a runner event as an agent decision. Its historical audit is the starting observation.

The stronger paper would identify when contradictory feedback causes a specific, reproducible failure; show whether independent evidence changes that response; quantify the tradeoff with repairing real defects; and test the result outside a small named-mode language. Its deliverable would be a validated experimental protocol and a bounded empirical finding. A new metric or benchmark matters only insofar as it exposes a decision that existing evaluations miss.

The most compelling possible takeaway is not that agents sometimes believe bad tests. It is that reliable repair requires choosing between changing the artifact and acquiring better evidence, and that this choice can be measured without rewarding either blind obedience or blanket inaction. Whether that claim survives the proposed tests remains open. Preserving that possibility of being wrong is what makes the direction scientifically worthwhile.
