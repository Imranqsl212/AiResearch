FOUNDATIONS SYNTHESIS — 16 November 2026
Companion to foundations_literature.json (C01–C50)

Scope and confidence
Fifty academic papers were verified through official scholarly records, publisher/proceedings pages, author-hosted manuscripts, institutional repositories, or arXiv. Forty-seven are peer-reviewed publications and three are explicitly labeled preprints. This is a targeted interdisciplinary review, not a systematic review. Most entries are abstract/metadata reviews; selected crucial papers were inspected more deeply. The bibliography states the access depth per source. Source findings, manuscript interpretations, and proposed hypotheses must remain separate.

What the current manuscript can establish
The strongest existing result is a measurement failure: the public endpoint and the raw finite tests can disagree because a hidden veto intervenes between them. This is not proof that a candidate is secure in the broader sense. It is also not yet evidence that the misleading verdict caused the agent to damage an earlier secure candidate: missing per-action raw outcomes block that temporal attribution. The historical archive is valuable for discovery and auditing; it cannot retrospectively supply missing counterfactuals. The current text should resist shifting between a benchmark audit, an agent-psychology claim, and a stopping-theory contribution without evidence for each.

The striking question is: When should a repair agent change its program, and when should it question the evidence judging that program? The question becomes scientifically useful only when both choices can be wrong. Correct-code-only evaluation rewards a policy that never edits. Initially defective controls make excessive distrust visible. Separating unauthorized acceptance from denial of legitimate requests prevents reject-all programs from appearing safe merely because they block everything.

Causal estimands and the secure-onset issue (C01–C05)
Let E mark eligibility/onset, determined before feedback intervention; Z be randomized feedback; H be the frozen candidate/history; and Y(z) be an outcome under intervention z. Then tau_E = E[Y(false)-Y(truthful) | E=1] is a coherent conditional effect. Selecting secure onset before randomization does not, by itself, introduce treatment-selection bias. It restricts the target population. If precursor models generate different eligible states, cross-model comparisons conflate continuation behavior with state composition. Use a common candidate bank or cross precursor-origin with continuation model; report natural self-generated states separately.

Distinguish three populations: (1) fixed selected states in the experiment, (2) a distribution of candidate states from a stated pipeline, and (3) unseen repositories or task families. Random assignment identifies contrasts for the first; wider claims require justified sampling/transport assumptions. Independent repeated runs estimate variation over model sampling, not independent variation over task families. A common random seed couples stochastic continuations and may improve precision; it does not create randomized assignment or representative sampling.

For an end-to-end pipeline, report onset probability and failures before onset alongside the conditional continuation effect. If the same downstream intervention is never reached in E=0 episodes and cannot affect them, its pipeline contribution equals P(E=1) times the conditional effect for that fixed upstream pipeline. Changing upstream policies invalidates that simplification. Do not use principal stratification merely because a subset is selected; it matters when eligibility is itself post-treatment relative to the intervention being compared.

Design and inference (C06–C14, C29)
Randomization unit, sampling unit, and unit of generalization must be stated separately. Task families, candidates, seeds, and repeated checker calls form a hierarchy; 1,000 calls on five families are not 1,000 independent security problems. Pre-register family-level primary summaries plus candidate-paired contrasts; show each family. Where treatments are randomized within candidate/model blocks, permutation or randomization inference must preserve those blocks and the actual assignment mechanism. If both potential branches are exhaustively run, the fixed-state paired difference is directly observed; arbitrary label swapping alone is not a justified sampling distribution without an exchangeability or randomized-assignment argument.

Mixed models are an option, not an automatic cure. Barr et al. motivate accounting for design-supported variation; Matuschek et al. demonstrate power tradeoffs from unnecessary complexity. Few family clusters support limited precision and weak population claims regardless of seed count. Power planning should simulate the intended hierarchy, plausible effect sizes, and missingness. Repeated pilots require a held-out confirmatory corpus and frozen primary hypotheses; confidence sequences address suitable optional-stopping questions but do not repair adaptive benchmark design or biased eligibility.

Minimum convincing factorial study
Cross initial code state (correct/defective) with verdict reliability, holding candidate/history fixed within pairs. Add truthful PASS and truthful FAIL controls, false FAIL on correct code, and false PASS on defective code; these distinguish harmful overreaction from harmful complacency. Cross feedback explanation validity independently of source branding where feasible. Keep information quantity and continuation opportunities matched. A blank or absent receipt can change the agent's decision problem; a neutral receipt is a useful control but must be treated as a defined intervention, not as interchangeable with truthful feedback.

Give every branch comparable preserve, edit, audit, and escalate options under the same action budget; otherwise forced continuation can manufacture regression. The optional second-opinion tool already exists in the project's extension plan. Its useful extension is to measure selective auditing across checker accuracy, error correlation, and cost, including a defective-code population in which blanket skepticism fails. Record exact delivered bytes, candidate state before and after every action, raw suite outcomes, public outcomes, and independent security/functionality outcomes. Evaluate the strength of the independent evaluator; it remains another operationalization, not metaphysical ground truth.

Measurement and decision outcomes (C15–C21, C30–C36)
Use an outcome vector: unauthorized acceptance U, legitimate denial D, successful repair R, and resource cost C. Report separate transitions from baseline, temporary damage, final damage, and recovery. State how request-level rates aggregate into candidate and family-level outcomes. A single regression bit can conceal opposite changes in U and D. Stakeholder weights can yield a scalar utility for a specified deployment, but sensitivity analyses should show when policy rankings reverse.

Noninferiority is appropriate only for a prespecified tolerated loss in repair effectiveness or functionality, with a meaningful margin and adequate power. Failure to find a significant loss is not noninferiority. A policy claiming reduced unsafe regressions while preserving repair success needs an upper bound on the security loss or a superiority contrast, and a separate confidence bound demonstrating the repair-success margin. Define multiplicity handling, missing-outcome treatment, and the unit behind each interval. Report absolute risk differences and paired counts, not only odds ratios or p values.

Unexpected but grounded mechanisms
1. Evidence reversal, not generic trust: a persuasive explanation may increase reliance even when its evidence is invalid. Human automation studies motivate this possibility (C37–C42) but do not establish it for LLMs. Randomize authority cues and falsifiable counterexample validity independently. Outcome: does the agent inspect evidence or merely defer to presentation? A null would reject the suggested mechanism rather than invalidate the benchmark.
2. Safety/availability substitution: false FAIL might reduce unauthorized acceptance while increasing legitimate denial. The current CSRF observations make this worth testing, not proved generally. Evaluate positive as well as negative security obligations. An agent that becomes more conservative is different from one that becomes less secure.
3. Optimization amplifies evaluator defects: increased budget might first improve repair and later damage independent performance, depending on checker quality. Reward-corruption and proxy-optimization work motivate this hypothesis (C43–C47). A monotone beneficial curve would bound the mechanism's scope; nonmonotonicity alone is insufficient evidence without ruling out timeouts, task difficulty, and candidate-selection changes.
4. Selective preservation: rollback/shield baselines may prevent known damage more cheaply than elaborate reasoning (C23, C48–C50). They may also block useful repairs or exploit evaluator access unavailable to the agent. Match information and measure useful repair, not just preservation. Distinguish a deployable policy from an evaluator-only upper bound.

Novelty boundaries
C24–C28 establish partial-observation planning and computation selection; C30–C33 establish reject options and confidence/coverage tradeoffs. Costed verification, a stop/verify/act policy, and a new name for them are not new scientific contributions. AMV-Guard (C34; October 2026 preprint) already explores action-conditioned verification, candidate-fixed interventions, noisy checks, and information-matched reference planning. The project must distinguish its intervention and outcomes from that work.

A defensible opportunity is a repair-specific causal benchmark of evidence-induced changes to executable artifacts, with symmetry between initially correct and defective code, explicit security-versus-functionality transitions, and an information-matched policy frontier. Even this conjunction is a candidate contribution, not a novelty theorem. Adjacent APR/testing and LLM-agent evidence must be integrated before making priority claims.

Ranked strategy
First: repair-specific causal measurement with symmetric controls and independently validated outcomes. Highest feasibility and clearest falsifiability; scientific impact depends on replication across diverse families and models.
Second: evidence-quality versus authority mechanism. Potentially more surprising, but requires careful matched messages and checks that models actually receive equivalent information.
Third: selective audit/preserve/edit policy. Strong practical value if it beats simpler baselines on both repair and risk; weakest generic novelty because relevant decision frameworks and AMV-Guard already exist.
Fourth: formal impossibility or identifiability result for correlated faulty feedback. Potentially high conceptual impact, but only if assumptions connect to executable cases and the theorem exceeds established corrupted-reward and partial-observation results.

Writing and narrative
Lead with the decision conflict and a concrete observed discrepancy; state its scope immediately. Present the archival audit as the motivation and measurement prerequisite. Put cohort bookkeeping and failed-admission ledgers in appendices while keeping exclusions transparent. Organize related work around claims that existing literature does and does not settle. Write proposed results as hypotheses and distinguish experiments already completed from future work. A strong conclusion would state which feedback-induced transitions were established, which decision policy improved the measured tradeoff, and where the evidence fails to generalize. No available result justifies promising acceptance at a top-tier venue.
