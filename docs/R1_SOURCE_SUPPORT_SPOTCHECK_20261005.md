# R1 candidate source-support and leakage spotcheck — 5 October 2026

**Scope.** Independent, read-only spotcheck of the 20 rows in [`retrieval_corpus_r1_20_candidate.jsonl`](../benchmark/retrieval_corpus_r1_20_candidate.jsonl) against their linked primary pages and the corresponding public C1 task cards. I also inspected finite candidate modes and evaluator code where a paragraph appeared to narrow the answer. This is **not** either of the two required blind source/relevance/leakage reviews, a source snapshot, a family admission, or an R1 freeze. The [review register](R1_TWENTY_REVIEW_REGISTER_20261005.csv) remains entirely `PENDING`.

**Method and status.** On 5 October 2026, the web reader opened 19/20 cited primary pages directly and examined the official MITRE search extract for CWE-22 after its direct page returned an internal error. It also opened the NIST PDFs rather than relying on their landing-page abstracts. Thus the `path-boundary` row has only partial direct-page verification. No exact page-byte snapshot/hash was saved. NIST final PDFs and RFC 8725 are versioned publications, whereas the OWASP, Redis, GitHub and `/latest/` Unicode URLs can change. The current Unicode UTS #39 page identifies version 18.0.0, revision 34 and points to [the versioned report](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr39/tr39-34.html); its current URL is still `/reports/tr39/`. MITRE pages displayed CWE 4.20 but the URLs do not pin 4.20. These access observations are not substitute source-snapshot hashes.

In the table, **topical** means the page supports the short general mechanism, **partial** means the page does not establish the finite task's exact rule, and **citation repair** means the cited page is materially less apt than another primary page. “Answer cue” identifies a reviewer question, not a finding that hidden evaluator facts leaked. The public task cards already disclose titles, starter modes and, for many rows, candidate mode names.

## Repair before any proposed R1 admission

1. Fix the specific citation/claim mismatches first: `environment_injection` CWE-426 versus CWE-427; `unicode_identity` UAX #15 versus UTS #39; `transaction_replay` credential replay versus transaction-ID deduplication; `key_rotation` scoped old-key policy; `csrf_origin` token-only wording; `ambiguous_parameters` risk versus blanket duplicate rejection. Rewrites would require new corpus/document hashes and a new excluded candidate identity; preserve this candidate unchanged as prior pilot input.
2. Ask two independent reviewers to compare **each of the 20 paragraphs** with the C1–C5 public card, all finite modes, secure reference, impossibility proof, and primary source. Record source support, relevance, whether the paragraph gives away an exact finite answer, and a disposition in the existing register. This spotcheck cannot pre-fill those judgments.
3. Preserve a permissible exact source version or review snapshot with its hash for each cited page. Use the NIST final PDF and RFC section identities; consider Unicode's versioned UTS/UAX links. Record the checked OWASP/Redis/GitHub/CWE revisions, since their current URLs alone do not reproduce today's content.
4. Recheck the treatment after any rewrite. A one-document-per-family corpus with domain filtering is curated guidance; it estimates the effect of those delivered excerpts and context length, not open-web RAG retrieval quality. The provider and main gate remain unadmitted.

**Limit:** The 19 direct page reads plus one official search extract establish at most topical support at the time of access. They do not independently establish the finite oracles, prove absence of semantic leakage, or yield an eligible main result.
