Dear Editor,

Please consider this exploratory empirical report for [journal/article type]. The study audited a completed local security-repair experiment: 40 episodes from one coding agent across five selected task families, with a separate eight-episode AEAD pilot. The central result concerns benchmark measurement. In 9 of 20 nominally unavailable main episodes, the final candidate passed the implemented family tests but was rejected by a hidden acceptance condition. Consequently, those trajectories cannot establish that continued attempts reflected an agent's failure to stop on a genuinely impossible repair. The manuscript provides the checker and receipt audit, a transparent accounting of the episodes, and explicit limits on what source changes, finite test passes, and final claims can support.

This paper contributes a concrete evaluator-validity audit for security-repair stopping studies. It does not claim a causal feedback effect, broad vulnerability-repair performance, or priority over existing benchmark-validity research. A later, separately labeled 30-episode feasibility pilot implements a bounded corrected oracle with three seeds per family-condition cell. Truthful contradiction feedback did not reduce submissions in its six matched unavailable-task blocks; one selected matched continuation changed after a false verdict. These results are descriptive and are not pooled with the historical denominator. The manuscript includes limitations of the finite security checks and a protocol for a larger independently validated study.
